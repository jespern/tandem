# Review: implementation findings against the frozen draft (Fable)

For: the `testbed` session and Jesper. Against `DESIGN.md` at `f4ce2e6`, `sdk/go` (`merge.go`, `device/rotate.go`, `device/device_test.go`), `proofs/README.md` (F1–F3), `vectors/README.md`, and the scenarios cited. 2026-11-12.

Format per item: verdict, why, then the text to put in `DESIGN.md` with its §. "Replace" means the quoted sentence goes; "add" means new text at the end of the named paragraph.

## Numbered findings

### 1. Resuming an unexported key change kills the collection. Real; the Go fix is right, the boundary is wrong.

Confirmed in `rotate.go` (`Resume`, step 4) and `TestCrashThenSecondDevice/2:journal`. §8.2 ("after a crash it resumes with those bytes") and §11.6 ("never abandons signed bytes it has exported") contradict §9.4 and §11.6's own draft rule: an unannounced statement "is not a successor, and not one half of a conflict". If a draft isn't a successor, abandoning it costs nothing, and the only bytes a device must never abandon are the ones it has **announced**.

Answer to the question: "exported" is the wrong boundary. The note does not export the signed bytes (it carries the statement ID), and even step 3's upload exports them only into a channel that only the owner's devices can read. The boundary is the announcement. What must hold is (a) no device announces a draft whose parent is no longer the head or whose note is gone, and (b) an abandoned draft is destroyed, so no other device can "finish" it later while its author has signed a replacement. (b) is what makes a second signature on the same parent safe.

One race the Go fix needs a sentence for: A abandons `S_A`, signs `S_A'` and writes a new note; B, which had read the old note, finds `S_A`'s channel gone and takes the "discard" path, removing the note, which now names `S_A'`. Finishing and discarding must be conditional on the note still naming the statement the device read.

**§8.2, replace the second bullet:**

> Before signing, a device syncs the roster and home. It never **announces** two different successors of one statement. It persists the exact signed bytes in its journal before writing the note or exporting anything, and after a crash resumes with those bytes while its note is still in home naming its statement and the statement's parent is still the roster's head. Until it is announced a statement is a draft (§9.4) and may be abandoned, by the device that signed it or by another device of the owner (§11.7): its channel is destroyed, its note removed and its journal entry dropped, and the user is told. A device signs a second successor of one statement only after it has abandoned the first.

**§11.6, step 2, add:** "The journal is written before the note. A note in home that names a statement this device has no journal for is treated as another device's note (§11.7)."

**§11.6, step 4, replace the first sentence with:**

> **Announce:** sync, and check that the note in home still names this statement and that its parent is still the roster's head. If either fails, another device discarded or overtook the change (§11.7): destroy the new channel, drop the journal and tell the user that nothing changed. Otherwise publish the statement in the **old** epoch channel's `_roster`. This is the moment the change happens, and the only evidence that it did. Then move the key from the note into `keys` in home and remove the note.

**§11.6, replace** "The device resumes the **same** change after a restart; it never abandons signed bytes it has exported to sign something else." **with:**

> After a restart the device syncs first. If its statement is announced, it carries on from step 5. If its note is still in home naming its statement and the parent is still the head, it resumes the same change from the journalled step. Otherwise (no note of its own, another device's note, or a new head) the change was discarded or overtaken: it drops the journal, destroys what it uploaded, and tells the user. A device that journalled a statement and crashed before writing its note keeps it only under the same two conditions; unannounced bytes may always be dropped.

**§11.7, add to the first bullet:** "It finishes a change only while the note still names that statement, and makes the check of step 4 before announcing." **Add to the second bullet:** "It removes the note only if it still names the statement it read, and for 'do it here' it signs only after the discard is on the hub." **Replace the last paragraph:**

> Two things can still put two successors on one statement, and each kills the collection (§9.4): the hub hides one device's change from another; or two devices pass the check of step 4 in the same moment, each announcing before it has seen the other's announcement. The check narrows that window to one round trip; it cannot close it (Q20). Members never end up following different rosters.

### 2. Which register a `delete` belongs to. Real; the proposed text is sound but leaves §12.3's promise unmet.

F1 is right: §9.2 says nothing, `owner_model.py` keys by (record, field), Lean and Go give the delete its own register (`Model.lean` `slot`, `merge.go` `slotOf`). Both must agree, and "only a delete supersedes a delete" is what keeps `deleted_sticky`.

Where I'd go further: with the delete in a register of its own, a deleted record's other writes are never superseded, so they are kept, published, carried through every inventory and healed for good. §12.3 promises "one small message per deleted record". The asymmetric rule gives that: a delete supersedes every write to the record it includes, in any field; nothing but a delete supersedes a delete. It is still a strict partial order (inclusion is transitive, and a non-delete can't reach a delete), `deleted_sticky` still holds, and a tombstone that saw the record shrinks it to one write. Writes concurrent with the delete stay as kept writes under the tombstone; they are rare and small. Lean and Go would change `slot`; if Jesper would rather not re-prove, the narrower rule is acceptable and the §12.3 sentence must then say "the deleted record's writes".

**§9.2, replace the Delete paragraph:**

> **Delete (op `delete`): sticky tombstone.** A delete write has an empty field name, no element and an empty value (`H("")` in `sigInput`), so every SDK signs it the same way. Its `context` and `base` are built over all kept writes of the record, in every field. A delete supersedes any write to the same record that it includes, whatever the field; a delete is superseded only by another delete that includes it. A record with any valid `delete` write is deleted, whatever else happens to it, and stays deleted (§12.3). Undeleting means creating a new record. (This is "delete wins", a deliberate product choice: an offline device editing a record someone deleted doesn't resurrect it.) `delete` is not used on the roster or named records (§7.3).

**§9.2, add after the Set paragraph:** "Registers are keyed by (record, field) for `assign`, (record, field, element) for `add`/`remove`, and the record alone for `delete`. An `assign` and an `add`/`remove` to one field name are different registers and never supersede each other; what the app makes of a field that holds both is the app's business."

### 3. Randomized signatures. Real; text as proposed, plus one line on identity.

CryptoKit is randomized; Go is deterministic. Nothing in the protocol depends on the signature bytes except §11.6's "the same bytes" and §9.4's "different", and §10.7's "every write in `m` is in `n`" should say by what.

**§5.5, add after `writeID = H(sigInput)`:** "A write is identified by its write ID. Ed25519 signing may be randomized (Apple's CryptoKit is), so one `sigInput` can carry different valid signatures from different devices of one user. Two writes with one write ID are one write, whichever signature arrived first; devices deduplicate by write ID and never compare signatures or envelopes to decide identity (§10.7)."

**§9.4, replace** "Receipt of any two different `_roster` successors of the same statement" **with** "Receipt of any two `_roster` successors of the same statement with different statement IDs".

**§11.6, replace** "gets the same bytes and the same write ID" **with** "gets the same `sigInput` and the same write ID; the signature bytes may differ (§5.5)".

### 4. Inventory entries with no writes. Real; as proposed.

**§8.3, add after "Record IDs are unique."** "Every entry lists at least one write; a record with nothing to carry over does not appear. The bytes are then a function of the set of (record, write) pairs."

### 5. "A valid roster statement". Real; as proposed, and say what an invalid owner-signed statement does to the chain.

**§8.3, replace the first sentence:** "A data write names a roster statement that is valid, in a chain of valid statements from the genesis (§8.2), and is signed by its author, whom that statement lists as owner or writer."

**§9.4, add to "Following the roster":** "An owner-signed successor that is invalid under §8.2 is kept, counts for the conflict test, and has no successors: the chain ends at its parent and no data write may name it. The owner's device that signed it has a bug; the remedy is a copy (§11.8)."

### 6. Bytes the vectors had to choose. Confirm six, replace one, and specify the pairing JSON.

1. **AAD tag as b32 text.** Confirm. **§5.4, add to the `aad` line's comment:** "`tag` is the channel tag as its 32-byte b32 text (§5.6), the same bytes as the `Tandem-Channel` header."
2. **Genesis `n`, `key.n`, `nonce`.** Confirm. **§7.1, add:** "The genesis has `n: 0`, `key.n: 0`, no `key.prev`, no `snapshot`, and `nonce`: its 16 random bytes in b64u."
3. **Home record names in b64u.** Confirm. **§6.2, add:** "`<collectionID>` and `<inviteID>` in record names are spelled in b64u."
4. **Key request `have`.** Confirm. **§11.4, add:** "`have` is the 32-byte ID of the latest roster statement the member holds; the roster reply starts at its successor."
5. **Vectors in any JSON order.** Replace. §10.5 insists on one reading per message, and a vector whose JSON order differs from its signed order is a second spelling of the same write for no gain. **§10.5, add to the invalid list:** "a `c` or `b` vector is not sorted by device ID, as it is signed (§5.5), or names a device twice."
6. **`v` only on assign, `e` only on add/remove.** Confirm. **§10.5, add after the kind-1 example:** "`v` is present exactly on `assign`, `e` exactly on `add` and `remove`; a `delete` has `f: ""` and neither."
7. **HLC millis ≥ 2^48 invalid.** Confirm. **§9.1, add to the HLC paragraph:** "A write whose `millis` is 2^48 or more, or whose counter exceeds 65 535, is invalid."

**Pairing messages.** Real gap. Three messages share one channel and one kind; the reply must be JSON because §5.4 says every plaintext is. **§6.3, replace the three payloads:**

> step 1: `{ "type": "request", "name", "ek": "<b64u one-time public key>" }`; step 2: `{ "type": "challenge", "ek", "n": "<b64u 16 random bytes>", "sig" }`; step 4: `{ "type": "reply", "ek", "sealed": "<b64u enc || ct>" }`, the HPKE output of §5.4. A message of another `type`, or one whose `ek` the device doesn't recognise, is ignored. Join requests (§11.2) carry `"type": "join"`.

**`_name` on named records.** Decide it: `_roster` carries nothing but `s`. A `_name` write on `_roster` would be a data write to a reserved record, signed under some statement, which §8.2 and §8.3 both reject; and the genesis's `_name` would have to be signed before the collection ID exists. **§7.3, replace** "and carry their name in a `_name` field" **with** "and, except `_roster`, carry their name in a `_name` field, written with their first write. `_roster` has no field but `s`; any other write to it is invalid."

## Scenario gaps

**G-06, leave notice lost at a rotation (§11.5). Real.** The "current epoch" test exists to stop a notice from an earlier stay; the right test is that nobody removed the member between `at` and the head. The owner's devices already read earlier epochs' tags (§11.4). **§11.5, replace** "A notice counts only if `at` is a statement of the current epoch, so one from an earlier stay can't remove someone who has come back." **with** "A notice counts only if `at` is a statement of the roster that lists the member, and every statement from `at` to the head lists them too; a notice from an earlier stay has a removal in between and does not count. The owner's devices read leave notices in every epoch whose key home holds."

**G-26, a future-claiming context poisons `base` (§9.2, §11.6). Real, narrower than stated, and the scenario's fixes don't work.** The loss is confined to the one field M poisoned, in M's epoch: late honest writes to that field are neither signed again nor accepted when restored, because every new-epoch write's `base` copies `reach(m)`. Building `base` only from dots the device holds is unsound: an honest write whose superseded predecessors were pruned would never be covered either, and `m` could never be superseded at all. Excluding a removed author's claims doesn't help: honest E4 contexts already copied them. What the author's device *can* check is a claim about itself: a `base` entry for its own device ID above any dot it has issued is a forgery. **§11.6, replace** "It signs again any write of its own user that the inventory doesn't list and the new state doesn't supersede (§9.2)." **with** "It signs again any write of its own device that the inventory doesn't list and that no listed write supersedes under the old epoch's rules, and also one that the new state supersedes only through a vector entry for this device's ID higher than any dot this device has issued: that entry is forged (§9.3), and the write signed again survives as a sibling. A write that a listed write replaced is not signed again: that would bring the replaced value back on top." **§9.3, replace** "the next honest write settles the field" **with** "the next honest write settles the field, at the cost that late honest writes to that field from the poisoned epoch come back only when their author's device signs them again (§11.6)".

**G-37, set contexts per element (§9.2). Real.** **§9.2, "Building a write", replace** "over the kept writes `k` of the current epoch" **with** "over the kept writes `k` of the same register (the field for `assign`; the element for `add`/`remove`; the record for `delete`) in the current epoch", and the same qualifier on `base`. Per-field contexts break add-wins whenever a device holds a later dot of a device without an earlier one, which a split record (§10.6) allows.

**G-40, records split across messages (§10.6, §10.7). Real, and the Lean GC proof covers only the pairwise rule.** Two things are missing: a deterministic split and domination by a set. **§10.6, add:** "A message's scope is a prefix of write IDs, `"part": "<hex prefix>"` (absent: the whole record); it holds every kept write of the record whose ID starts with the prefix. A device splits by extending the prefix one hex digit at a time until each part fits, so every device splits a given state the same way. Sizes count the envelope (§5.4); the stored message is about 4/3 larger." **§10.7, replace the domination sentence:** "A set of messages `N` of the same record **dominates** a message `m` if the scopes in `N` cover `m`'s scope and every write in `m` is in a message of `N` or superseded by a write in one. A single `n` is the set `{n}`." Then: "A device MAY destroy `m` once it has merged it and the other messages on the hub dominate it; where `m` in turn belongs to a set that dominates a member `n` of `N`, it may do so only if `H(m)` is greater than `H(n)`." The pairwise tie-break is the special case; the Lean `GC` module should be extended to sets before this is frozen, and the republish trigger reads "messages for one record that no set of them dominates".

**G-60, forged version-2 message (§15). Real; the fix is structural, not textual.** A newer version must never write where a v1 client reads, so a v2 message under a v1 tag is by construction junk. **§15, replace the second and fourth bullets:**

> - A newer version never appends to a channel whose tag a v1 client derives: v2 tags use v2 labels. Moving a collection to v2 is a v1 roster statement that starts an epoch with `"v": 2`; v1 clients hold that statement, follow nothing after it, and tell the user an update is needed. Homes and inboxes move the same way, by a signed v1 home write naming the v2 home tag.
> - A message in a v1 channel whose header or envelope version isn't 1 is junk: ignored, logged, never destroyed, and no reason to pause healing or garbage collection. The two version fields are compared and must agree; the envelope byte decides what the message is, the header only what a client fetches.

**G-20, HLC durability (§9.1). Real.** Recovering an HLC from the hub is unreliable offline; the design already has the right tool. **§9.1, add:** "The HLC is persisted with the device ID, before any write made with it leaves the device, including to an extension (§17). A device that finds its HLC gone picks a new device ID (§5.3): a lost clock is a fresh installation, and old dots can then never collide with new ones."

**G-01, pairing messages never destroyed (§6.3). Real.** **§6.3, add after step 5:** "The new device erases the one-time private key as soon as the reply is opened, and at the QR's expiry if none came; from then on the sealed reply is unreadable by anyone. After step 5 it destroys its request and the reply; the showing device destroys its challenges when the QR expires or after approval. Leftovers are junk (§10.7) and harmless."

**G-49, destroyed requests re-posted by neither side (§12.2). Real, minor.** **§12.2, add a bullet:** "**A device's own pending request.** While a membership is `requested` and its invite unexpired, the joiner's device re-appends its join request if the hub lacks its envelope (§10.2); a pairing device does the same for its request until the QR expires or a challenge arrives. The showing device answers one `ek` once, however many times it sees it."

**G-09, refused requests get no answer (§11.3). Real.** A request must not carry a time: the owner's device would judge a photographed auto-approve QR by the attacker's clock. **§11.3, add:** "A request the device refuses without a tap gets `{ "type": "declined", "c", "i", "why": "expired" | "used" | "closed" }` and is destroyed; for a link invite the owner is told who asked and offered a new link." **§6.2, replace the `state` values with** "`requested`, `declined`, `active`, `left`, `removed`" **and §11.4's first receiver requirement with** "in state `requested`, `declined` or `active`". **§11.4, replace** "the client tells the user and keeps the request until the invite expires" **with** "the client tells the user, sets `state` to `declined`, and stops waiting; it does the same when the invite's `exp` passes. A valid grant still counts."

## Missed

- **§8.3 named-statement check on `_meta`** says "lists its author as the owner"; with finding 5 it should also be the chain rule. Covered by the §8.3 sentence above.
- **Same-epoch statements also need step 4's check.** Admitting a member (§11.3) signs and publishes a successor in one go; it should sync and check the head between the two, or the auto-approve race of RO-009 variant b is the pre-check gap again. One clause in §8.2's first bullet: "and, between signing and publishing, makes the check of §11.6 step 4".
- **G-19, provable junk.** A message under a collection tag that fails the AEAD under the epoch key is junk to every member; it could be destroyed by the device that proved it, with the same hash rule as copies. Leaving it is safe; destroying it is a one-line MAY in §10.7. Not needed for v1.
- **Decision log.** Each change above is a row in §19; the ones that alter proved modules (2, G-37, G-40) should name the Lean theorem to re-run.
